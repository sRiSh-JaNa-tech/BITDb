Consensus algorithms allow distributed systems to agree on values.
They are essential for maintaining consistent database state replicas.
Paxos is the classic consensus algorithm proposed by Leslie Lamport.
Paxos is notoriously difficult to understand and implement correctly.
Raft was designed as an alternative to Paxos, focusing on understandability.
Raft structures consensus around a leader election mechanism.
A Raft cluster has three states: Leader, Candidate, and Follower.
Followers receive heartbeats and log entries from the Leader.
Candidates initiate elections if leader heartbeats time out.
A candidate needs a majority of votes to become the Leader.
The Leader is responsible for managing the replicated log.
Log replication ensures all follower logs match the leader log.
Committing an entry requires replication to a majority of nodes.
Term numbers are used to detect stale leaders and candidates.
Safety guarantees ensure that committed entries are never modified.
Consensus algorithms operate correctly as long as a majority is functional.
They handle network partitions without violating safety properties.
During partition, the side without a majority stops committing.
Multi-Paxos optimizes basic Paxos for sequences of consensus decisions.
ZooKeeper Atomic Broadcast (ZAB) is another consensus protocol variant.
Byzantine Fault Tolerance (BFT) handles malicious or lying nodes.
Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (PBFT) is used in blockchain.
Consensus protocols require message exchanges that limit write throughput.
Optimizing consensus message round-trips is a key research area.
Distributed databases rely on these algorithms for strong consistency.
