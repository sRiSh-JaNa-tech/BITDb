SVM optimization finds the maximum margin separating hyperplane.
The primal formulation minimizes squared weights subject to constraints.
Lagrangian multipliers are introduced to handle inequality constraints.
The dual formulation is derived by setting primal derivatives zero.
SVM dual formulation maximizes dual variables subject to constraints.
The dual objective depends only on dot products of samples.
This dependency allows seamless integration of kernel functions.
Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions define optimal solution properties.
Complementary slackness dictates dual variables are non-zero only for support vectors.
Support vectors lie directly on or inside the margin boundaries.
Data points with zero dual variables do not affect the hyperplane.
Sequential Minimal Optimization (SMO) solves the dual QP problem.
SMO breaks the large QP problem into 2-variable subproblems.
Analytical solutions exist for these 2-variable subproblems.
This avoids expensive matrix operations during optimization steps.
SMO selects pairs of variables using heuristics to speed convergence.
C hyperparameter scales the penalty for constraint violations.
Soft-margin optimization uses slack variables to allow violations.
Hinge loss represents the objective function optimized by soft-margin.
Hinge loss is zero for correct classifications outside margin.
It increases linearly with distance inside margin or misclassification.
Subgradient descent can optimize primal formulation for linear SVMs.
Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) scales to very large datasets.
Pegasos algorithm is a popular fast solver for SVM optimization.
Understanding SVM math is a milestone in optimization theory.
