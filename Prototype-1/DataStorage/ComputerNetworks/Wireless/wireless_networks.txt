Wireless networks use electromagnetic waves to transmit data between devices without wires.
Wi-Fi is a family of wireless network protocols based on IEEE 802.11 standards.
Wireless access points (APs) connect wireless devices to adjacent wired ethernet networks.
Service Set Identifier (SSID) is the primary name associated with a wireless local network.
CSMA/CA (Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Avoidance) manages wireless channel sharing.
Devices listen to the channel before transmitting, sending RTS/CTS signals to avoid collisions.
Frequency bands used by Wi-Fi include 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, and the newer 6 GHz band.
The 2.4 GHz band offers longer range but is more susceptible to signal interference.
The 5 GHz band offers faster data speeds and less congestion but shorter signal range.
MIMO (Multiple-Input Multiple-Output) uses multiple antennas to transmit and receive data.
Beamforming focuses wireless signals directly toward specific receiving client devices.
WPA2 (Wi-Fi Protected Access 2) is a standard security protocol using AES encryption.
WPA3 introduces stronger encryption, individualized data encryption, and protection against guessing.
Cellular networks divide coverage areas into cells, each served by a local transceiver.
GSM and CDMA were major competing technologies in second and third-generation cellular systems.
LTE (Long-Term Evolution) is a standard for high-speed wireless communication for mobile devices.
5G networks offer higher data rates, reduced latency, and support for massive IoT connections.
Bluetooth is a short-range wireless technology used for exchanging data over short distances.
Zigbee is a low-power, low-data-rate wireless mesh network standard for home automation.
Ad-hoc networks consist of peer-to-peer wireless connections without centralized access points.
Wireless mesh networks connect multiple AP nodes dynamically to route data redundant paths.
Signal attenuation is the loss of signal strength as it propagates through physical space.
Multipath propagation occurs when wireless signals reflect off surfaces, creating multiple paths.
Fading is the variation in signal attenuation caused by obstacles or atmospheric changes.
Orthogonal Frequency-Division Multiplexing (OFDM) splits channels into subcarriers to reduce interference.
